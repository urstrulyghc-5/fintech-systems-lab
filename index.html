INFRASTRUCTURE / 02

Payment rails

Different instruments use different processing, messaging and settlement arrangements.

INSTANT ACCOUNT-TO-ACCOUNT

UPI

India's interoperable payment system supports transfers between participating bank accounts through supported apps and payment addresses.

Behind the experience
Payment app, UPI ecosystem participants, remitter and beneficiary banks.
IMMEDIATE TRANSFER

IMPS

An Indian interbank electronic transfer service designed for immediate payments, subject to participating institutions and applicable rules.

Typical use
Urgent bank-to-bank transfers.
BATCH PROCESSING

NEFT

An RBI-operated electronic funds transfer system that processes transactions in half-hourly batches and operates around the clock.

Typical use
Retail and business bank transfers.
REAL-TIME GROSS SETTLEMENT

RTGS

Processes transfers individually in real time. RBI-prescribed minimum transaction value is ₹2 lakh; participating-bank rules also apply.

Typical use
High-value transfers.
NETWORK-BASED

Card payments

A card purchase can involve merchant acceptance, an acquirer, a card network and an issuer. Authorisation, clearing and settlement are distinct.

Typical use
Online and in-store purchases.
STORED VALUE / ACCOUNT ACCESS

Wallets

A wallet may hold stored value or provide access to another funding source. Its actual payment path depends on the product design.

Typical use
App-based payments and stored value.
The lifecycle, availability, limits and settlement model differ by rail. This comparison is educational and does not guarantee a particular transaction's speed or outcome.

DECISION SYSTEMS / 04

Risk & compliance

Understand why a payment may be allowed, challenged, held or rejected.

01 / DATA QUALITY

Instruction validation

Check required fields, data formats, beneficiary information, amount limits and other rail-specific requirements.

02 / AUTHENTICATION

Customer verification

Where applicable, establish whether the customer is authorised to initiate the payment using the required authentication method.

03 / FRAUD SIGNALS

Transaction risk

Evaluate unusual patterns, velocity, device or account signals and other indicators relevant to the payment context.

04 / REGULATORY

Compliance screening

Apply relevant identity, sanctions, reporting and other regulatory controls according to the product and jurisdiction.

05 / DECISION

Allow, review or reject

A decision may permit processing, require additional checks, delay the instruction or decline it under the applicable rules.

06 / EVIDENCE

Monitoring and audit

Record relevant decisions and events so authorised teams can investigate exceptions and demonstrate control operation.

Real risk engines use complex policies and data. The simulator's risk scenario is a teaching example, not a real institution's decision or compliance assessment.
AFTER PROCESSING / 05

Settlement & reconciliation

Understand why a successful response, the movement of obligations and matching records are not the same thing.

Three distinct concepts

1
Authorisation or processing response

A system returns a decision or status under its particular rules. A response alone does not explain every subsequent financial event.

2
Clearing and settlement

Relevant messages or obligations are calculated, exchanged or discharged through the applicable arrangement.

3
Reconciliation

Records from relevant systems are compared. Differences are investigated and resolved through controlled processes.

A reconciliation exception

Imagine a merchant's order system and payment provider show different statuses for the same order.

Merchant order recordPayment pending
Provider's payment recordSuccess response
Next operational stepInvestigate and verify
Match transaction references, compare amounts and timestamps, check the authoritative status and document the resolution. Do not change records simply to make them agree.

Common causes of mismatches

TIMING

Different update cycles

Systems can receive and record events at different times.

IDENTITY

Reference mismatch

Different identifiers or missing references can make matching difficult.

AMOUNT

Financial difference

Fees, partial processing, duplicate entries or incorrect amounts may create exceptions.

EXCEPTION MANAGEMENT / 06

Failures & recovery

Different symptoms require different operational responses. A timeout is not automatically a failed payment.

DECLINED

A negative response

A transaction attempt has received a decline or rejection response.

First actionInspect the reason
ThenFollow the applicable recovery rules
A retry may or may not be appropriate. The cause and rail-specific rules matter.
UNKNOWN

A timeout or lost response

The caller did not receive a conclusive answer within the expected time.

First actionKeep the original reference
ThenVerify authoritative status
Avoid issuing a duplicate payment before checking the original transaction's status.
PENDING

Processing continues

An intermediate status does not establish final success or failure.

First actionCheck status history
ThenFollow the provider's process
MISMATCH

Records disagree

Two systems may have different information about the same payment.

First actionMatch transaction identifiers
ThenInvestigate and document
Recovery depends on the payment method, provider and applicable rules. This is operational education, not a universal retry procedure.
