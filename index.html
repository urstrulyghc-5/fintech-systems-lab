01 / HOW FINANCE CHANGED

Evolution is not a straight line.

Financial technology did not begin with a mobile app. It evolved as people tried to make finance more accessible, reliable, scalable and useful. Each new layer solved some problems and introduced others.

FROM LEDGERS
TO CONNECTED FINANCE
The important nuance

Legacy banks did not simply disappear. Many modern services depend on their licences, deposits, balance sheets, risk management and payment access, while newer technology changes how customers interact with them. Digital capability is increasingly a competitive and operational necessity, but adoption paths differ by institution and market.

What changed, and what did not?

THEN · COMMON PATTERN

Branch-led processes

Paper forms, face-to-face identity checks, business-hour constraints and manual hand-offs were common in many services.

  • Physical access mattered
  • Records could be fragmented
  • Reconciliation often involved manual work
NOW · DIGITAL PATTERN

Connected services

Apps and APIs can expose services around the clock, automate decisions and connect records across institutions.

  • Access can be more convenient
  • Systems can scale rapidly
  • Cyber risk, outages and data governance become central

FinTech is also products, not just infrastructure.

Explore what the customer uses, what the provider does behind the interface and the trade-offs that should be made visible.

FIRST, IDENTIFY THE TRANSACTION

Who is paying whom, and through what rail?

A business model describes the parties. A payment rail describes the infrastructure that carries the payment. One transaction can be B2B and cross-border, while using a bank transfer or a card network.

B2B

Business to business

A retailer pays a supplier, or a company pays a service provider.

Consider: invoices, approvals, reconciliation, working capital and FX for international trade.
B2C

Business to consumer

A merchant pays a refund or a company disburses a benefit to a customer.

Consider: scale, customer experience, fraud controls and refunds.
C2B

Consumer to business

A customer pays a shop, utility, school or online service.

Consider: checkout friction, acceptance cost, speed and proof of payment.
C2C / P2P

Person to person

One individual sends money to another, often using a bank-linked app.

Consider: ease of use, mistaken transfers, scams and recipient verification.
B2G / G2P

Government-linked payments

Businesses pay public authorities, or government programmes pay eligible recipients.

Consider: auditability, access, identity, privacy and delivery failures.
RETAIL / WHOLESALE

Payment scale and purpose

Retail payments are usually everyday customer or business payments. Wholesale payments often support high-value institutional and interbank activity.

Important: the exact definitions and systems vary by jurisdiction.
Potential advantages

Digital payments can improve convenience, speed, traceability, access and reconciliation.

Costs and limitations

Fees, outages, fraud, privacy exposure, exclusion, disputes and settlement timing still matter.

DATA IS PART OF THE PAYMENT

Privacy, consent and responsible data use

Payments generate sensitive information: identity details, counterparties, amounts, locations, devices and spending patterns. Good FinTech explains why each data item is needed and who can access it.

01 · Minimise

Collect only what is necessary for the stated purpose.

02 · Explain

Make consent, purpose and sharing understandable, not hidden in vague wording.

03 · Protect

Use appropriate encryption, access controls, monitoring and secure retention.

04 · Govern

Set rules for vendors, deletion, incidents, audits and lawful disclosures.

The trade-off: more data can support fraud detection and personalisation, but can also increase exposure, bias, surveillance and breach impact. Consent alone does not replace security, legal duties or data minimisation.

Innovation is a question, not a slogan.

New technology matters when it changes a real constraint or creates a new capability. It can also create fresh risks, dependencies and costs. Explore both sides.

A USEFUL WAY TO EVALUATE CHANGE

What problem is the innovation actually solving?

Ask what is better, for whom, at what cost, under which assumptions, and compared with what alternative. A new technology is not automatically more efficient, safer or fairer.

Transparent by design

This lab will not promote a technology simply because it is popular. Each topic should include what it does, what evidence supports its use, what can fail, who bears the risk and which questions remain unsettled.

How to read valuations

Public-company market capitalisation changes with share prices. Private-company valuations usually reflect a dated funding round, tender offer or secondary transaction and are not continuously traded prices. Figures are included only where a clear, dated reference is available; a missing value is preferable to a misleading estimate.

Valuation discipline

Do not compare private valuations directly with public market capitalisation without noting the difference in measurement. Valuations can change rapidly, reported figures may be estimates, and private transactions may involve different share classes or terms. Always follow the linked source and its date before quoting a figure.
