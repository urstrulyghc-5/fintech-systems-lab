ACCOUNT-TO-ACCOUNT

UPI

India's interoperable instant payment system enables payments between participating bank accounts through supported applications and payment addresses.

Typical use: everyday transfers, QR payments and merchant collections.
INSTANT TRANSFER

IMPS

An Indian interbank electronic funds transfer service designed for immediate payments, available subject to participating institutions and their rules.

Typical use: urgent account-to-account transfers.
BATCH-BASED TRANSFER

NEFT

An RBI-operated electronic funds transfer system that processes transactions in half-hourly batches and operates around the clock.

Typical use: retail and business bank transfers.
HIGH-VALUE TRANSFER

RTGS

Real Time Gross Settlement processes transfers individually in real time. It is intended for high-value transfers and has an RBI-prescribed minimum of ₹2 lakh.

Typical use: high-value business and institutional transfers.
CARD NETWORK

Card payments

A card transaction can involve the merchant, acquirer, card network, issuer, authorisation, clearing and settlement. Authorisation and final settlement are distinct events.

Typical use: online and in-store purchases.
STORED VALUE

Wallets

A wallet stores or accesses value under its own operating model. Some wallet payments use a card or bank transfer behind the scenes; the exact path depends on the product.

Typical use: app-based payments and stored-value transactions.
This is a simplified educational comparison, not a live service-level guarantee. Availability, limits, settlement arrangements and transaction rules depend on the rail and participating institutions.

What may happen behind the scenes?

01 / INSTRUCTION

Payment initiation

The sender provides beneficiary details, amount, purpose and other information required by the provider.

02 / CONTROLS

Compliance checks

The institutions and service providers apply relevant identity, sanctions, fraud, reporting and regulatory controls.

03 / FUNDING

Currency conversion

Where needed, the provider determines the applicable exchange rate and arranges the required currency funding.

04 / ROUTING

Correspondent or local rails

The transfer may use correspondent banking, local clearing systems, payment networks or specialised providers.

05 / SETTLEMENT

Inter-institution obligations

Institutions settle their obligations through the arrangements relevant to the route. This is not always the same instant as initiation.

06 / DELIVERY

Beneficiary credit

The receiving institution applies its checks and processes the credit. Timing and confirmation depend on the route and provider.

Not every transfer follows the same path. The corridor, currency, provider, purpose, funding method and receiving institution determine the actual workflow.

Movement of obligations

Settlement is not the same as authorisation

An authorisation indicates that a transaction has received a particular decision. Clearing calculates or communicates obligations. Settlement discharges obligations according to the relevant system's rules.

1
Instruction accepted

A request has been received and validated.

2
Payment processed or authorised

The rail or relevant institution processes the instruction.

3
Clearing and settlement

The applicable system calculates or discharges obligations.

4
Reconciliation

Records from different systems are compared and exceptions investigated.

Operational control

Reconciliation

Reconciliation checks whether records agree across systems. A mismatch may arise from timing, missing records, duplicate entries, incorrect amounts or unresolved status.

Example exception

A payment provider records a successful response, but the merchant's order system has not updated. The operations team compares references, amounts, timestamps and authoritative payment status before resolving the mismatch.

Good reconciliation identifies differences; investigation establishes why they occurred; controlled resolution documents what was done.
DECLINED

The transaction was rejected

A decline is a negative response for that attempt. The next step depends on the reason, rail and authoritative status.

  • Capture the response or reason code where available.
  • Check whether the issue is correctable.
  • Retry only when permitted and appropriate.
UNKNOWN

The request timed out

The caller did not receive a conclusive response in time. The payment may still have been processed.

  • Retain the original transaction reference.
  • Query the provider or rail for authoritative status.
  • Avoid creating a duplicate payment before checking the original.
PENDING

Processing is not complete

Some workflows have intermediate states. A pending status should not automatically be treated as either success or failure.

  • Follow the provider's status-check process.
  • Respect applicable time limits and operating rules.
  • Escalate unresolved exceptions through the correct channel.
MISMATCH

Records do not agree

Different systems may update at different times or record different aspects of the same transaction.

  • Match stable transaction identifiers.
  • Compare amounts, timestamps and status history.
  • Document corrections and preserve an audit trail.
The correct recovery action depends on the payment rail and provider's documented process. Never assume a timeout means the payment failed.

A simplified infrastructure map

Who may participate in a payment?

The parties differ by payment method. This is a conceptual map, not a claim that every payment passes through every component.

01

Customer or payer

Initiates the payment through an application, bank channel, card or other instrument.

02

Merchant or beneficiary

Receives the payment instruction or funds, depending on the payment arrangement.

03

Application or provider

Collects information and may manage payment orchestration, customer experience and status.

04

Issuer or sending bank

May authenticate the customer, evaluate the transaction and provide or debit funds.

05

Network or payment system

Routes, exchanges or processes instructions according to its rules and technical design.

06

Acquirer or receiving bank

May support merchant acceptance, receive payment messages or credit the beneficiary.

07

Risk and compliance

Applies relevant fraud, identity, sanctions and regulatory controls.

08

Settlement infrastructure

Supports the discharge of obligations between participating institutions.

09

Reconciliation and operations

Compares records, investigates exceptions and maintains operational evidence.

For example, a UPI transfer and a card purchase have different participants and processing arrangements. Use the relevant payment-rail view rather than treating this map as one universal sequence.
